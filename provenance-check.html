    How it works

    The tool picks out thousands of distinctive points in each image (corners, edges, texture) and describes each one in a way that survives resizing and compression. It then looks for a single scale, rotation and position that lines up many of those points at once. Unrelated photos produce almost no consistent alignments, so dozens of aligned points mean the copy was made from your picture.

    Once aligned, your original is reprojected onto the copy, tone is matched, and the remaining differences are mapped. That is how added text, logos or retouching show up.

    Limits

    • This is a comparison aid, not a legal opinion. Consult a lawyer or your national photographers' association before acting on it.
    • It handles crops, resizing, mirroring, small rotations, compression and colour or tone edits. Heavy perspective warps, strong stylisation or AI re-rendering can defeat it.
    • Very small copies (thumbnails under about 200 px) or near-featureless scenes such as plain sky may give an inconclusive result.
    • Browsers can't open RAW or HEIC files. Export a full-size JPEG of your original first, and keep the RAW as your primary proof of authorship.
